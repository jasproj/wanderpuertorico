Puerto Rico Festivals & Events: Month-by-Month Calendar

Updated August 2026 · 12-minute read

Puerto Rico celebrates more per square mile than anywhere in the Caribbean. Between the island's 78 municipalities — each with its own patron saint fiesta — and the national festivals that draw hundreds of thousands, there's something happening every single week of the year. Some travelers plan entire trips around these events, and it's easy to see why: this is where Puerto Rican culture is at its loudest, most colorful, and most welcoming.

Here's every major festival and event worth planning around, organized by month.

January

Fiestas de la Calle San Sebastián (SanSe)

Mid-JanuaryOld San JuanFree300,000+ attend

Puerto Rico's biggest party — and it's not close. Four days and nights of live music stages (salsa, reggaetón, bomba y plena, rock en español), food vendors lining every block, artisan markets, vejigante mask parades, and an entire city district turned into an open-air celebration. Calle San Sebastián and the surrounding streets become a wall-to-wall crowd by evening. Book accommodation months in advance — Old San Juan hotels sell out. Getting there: walk or take the free trolley. Don't drive.

Three Kings Day (Día de los Reyes)

January 6Island-wideFree

More important than Christmas Day in Puerto Rican tradition. Children receive gifts, families gather for feasts, and towns hold parades and celebrations. The biggest celebration is in Juana Díaz, the "City of the Three Kings," with elaborate processions and live music. A quieter cultural event than SanSe, but deeply meaningful.

February – March

Carnaval de Ponce

Week before Ash WednesdayPonceFree

The island's largest carnival — a week of parades, music, dancing, and the famous vejigante characters in elaborate papier-mâché masks chasing children with inflated bladders. The final parade on the last day is the main event. Ponce transforms completely. The masks alone are worth the trip — this is where the tradition originates. Combine with a visit to Ponce's museums and architecture.

Coffee Harvest Festival (Festival de la Cosecha de Café)

FebruaryMaricaoFree

Celebrates the end of coffee harvest season in Puerto Rico's coffee country. Local haciendas offer tastings, there's live jíbaro music (traditional mountain folk music), food vendors serving mountain cuisine, and coffee-picking demonstrations. Small-town feel, very local, and a chance to buy fresh-roasted coffee direct from growers. The drive to Maricao through the mountains is half the experience.

Casals Festival

Late February – MarchSan JuanTicketed

Classical music festival founded by cellist Pablo Casals, who lived in Puerto Rico. Orchestral performances, chamber music, and guest soloists at venues across San Juan. The most prestigious cultural event on the island's calendar. Tickets range from $20-$75 depending on the performance.

April – May

Saborea Puerto Rico

AprilSan Juan (Escambrón Beach)Ticketed

The island's premier food and wine festival. Top chefs from Puerto Rico and guest chefs from the mainland set up tasting stations on the beach. Cooking demonstrations, cocktail competitions, and local food vendors. Three days of eating and drinking on the waterfront. Tickets sell out — buy early. This is where you taste the cutting edge of Puerto Rican cuisine. Read our food guide to prep your palate.

Heineken Jazz Fest

MaySan JuanTicketed

Three-day jazz festival featuring Latin jazz, Caribbean rhythms, and international acts. Multiple stages, food vendors, and a beach-adjacent setting. One of the top jazz festivals in the Caribbean. Draws both serious jazz fans and casual music lovers. Evening performances run late.

June

Noche de San Juan (St. John's Eve)

June 23-24Island-wide beachesFree

The most magical night of the year. At midnight on June 23, Puerto Ricans wade into the ocean and fall backwards into the waves — tradition says it brings good luck and cleanses bad energy. Beaches across the island fill with families, bonfires, music, and food. Condado Beach and Isla Verde in San Juan have the biggest gatherings. It's spontaneous, joyful, and entirely unique to Puerto Rico. Show up at any public beach around 10 PM and join in.

July – August

Fiestas Patronales (Patron Saint Festivals)

Year-round, peak in summerEvery municipalityFree

Every one of Puerto Rico's 78 towns celebrates its patron saint with a multi-day fiesta — live music (often big-name acts), food vendors, carnival rides, religious processions, casetas (small bars set up in the town plaza), and the entire community in the streets. Summer has the highest concentration. These are the most authentic cultural experiences on the island — not tourist events, but real community celebrations where visitors are genuinely welcome. Check local calendars for dates.

Loíza Festival (Fiestas de Santiago Apóstol)

Late JulyLoízaFree

One of the most important Afro-Caribbean cultural celebrations in Puerto Rico. Loíza's patron saint festival features vejigante characters wearing coconut-shell masks (distinct from Ponce's papier-mâché style), bomba y plena drumming and dancing, colorful processions, and traditional food. This is where Puerto Rico's African heritage is most visible and celebrated. Raw, loud, and deeply cultural.

September – October

Hurricane Season Lull

Sep–OctIsland-wideFewer events

The peak of hurricane season means fewer scheduled festivals. Some patron saint fiestas still happen, but major outdoor events are usually scheduled outside this window. This is the cheapest time to visit, but the tradeoff is less cultural activity and some weather risk. Indoor venues (museums, galleries, restaurants) operate normally.

November – December

Festival de Bomba y Plena

NovemberVarious locationsFree/Ticketed

Celebrating Puerto Rico's signature musical traditions — bomba (African-rooted call-and-response drumming with dance) and plena (narrative folk music called "the newspaper of the people"). Performances, workshops, and community events across the island. If you've never seen live bomba, the interplay between the dancer and the lead drummer is electrifying — the drummer follows the dancer's movements, not the other way around.

Christmas Season (Navidades)

Late Nov – Mid-JanuaryIsland-wideFree

Puerto Rico has the longest Christmas season in the world — it starts in late November and doesn't end until mid-January (after SanSe). Parrandas (Puerto Rican caroling — a group shows up at your door with instruments, you feed them and join the group to the next house) happen nightly. Towns put up elaborate decorations. Restaurants serve holiday specialties (coquito, lechón, pasteles, arroz con gandules). The energy is contagious — even if you're not religious, the communal celebration is infectious.

Hatillo Masks Festival (Festival de las Máscaras)

December 28HatilloFree

An all-day festival where locals dress in elaborate costumes and masks, parading through the streets of this north-coast town. The tradition commemorates the story of King Herod. Hundreds of masked characters, live music, and food. Less well-known than Ponce Carnival but equally colorful and more intimate.

Planning Tips

Book Early for Big Festivals

SanSe (January) and Ponce Carnival (February/March) sell out hotels weeks in advance. Noche de San Juan (June) fills beachside properties. For patron saint fiestas in small towns, accommodation options are limited year-round — consider staying in San Juan and driving to the festival.

Getting There

For San Juan festivals (SanSe, Saborea), walk or use Uber. Parking is impossible. For festivals outside San Juan (Ponce Carnival, Loíza, Maricao), rent a car. Rural festivals have limited or no rideshare coverage. Designate a driver — these events involve drinking.

What to Expect

Puerto Rican festivals are loud, late, and genuinely welcoming to outsiders. Music starts mid-afternoon and runs past midnight. Food vendors accept cash and most take cards. Dress casual — shorts and comfortable shoes. Hydrate — outdoor festivals in the tropics are hot. The best cultural moments happen when you stop watching and start participating.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the biggest festivals in Puerto Rico?

The biggest are Fiestas de la Calle San Sebastián (January, Old San Juan — 300,000+ attendees), Carnaval de Ponce (February, the island's largest carnival), Saborea food festival (April), and the numerous patron saint fiestas that happen in every town throughout the year.

What is the best time to visit Puerto Rico for festivals?

January through March is the peak festival season — San Sebastián, Ponce Carnival, and Coffee Harvest festivals all fall in this window. June has the Noche de San Juan celebrations. But every month has patron saint fiestas in different towns — there's always something happening.

What is Fiestas de la Calle San Sebastián?

Puerto Rico's biggest street festival, held in mid-January on Calle San Sebastián in Old San Juan. Four days of live music (salsa, reggaetón, bomba y plena), food vendors, artisan markets, parades with vejigante masks, and hundreds of thousands of people in the streets. Free to attend.

Are Puerto Rico festivals free?

Most are free. Street festivals, patron saint fiestas, and public celebrations like San Sebastián and Noche de San Juan cost nothing to attend. Some specific concerts or events within larger festivals may charge admission. Food and drinks are pay-as-you-go from vendors.

What is a patron saint fiesta in Puerto Rico?

Every town in Puerto Rico has a patron saint, and each celebrates with a multi-day fiesta patronal — live music, food vendors, carnival rides, religious processions, and community gatherings. There are 78 municipalities, so fiestas happen year-round across the island. They're the most authentic cultural experience available to visitors.
