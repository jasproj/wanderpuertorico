Puerto Rico vs Bahamas: Bio Bays, Pink Sand & the Real Cost Difference

Updated August 2026 · 3 min read

Two Caribbean classics, very different experiences. Puerto Rico is a U.S. territory with rainforests, history, and glowing bays. The Bahamas is an independent archipelago with the clearest water in the Western Hemisphere and swimming pigs. Here is what matters.

The Bahamas has clear water. Puerto Rico has water that glows.

Passport & Logistics

Puerto Rico: U.S. territory. No passport, no customs, no currency exchange. Your cell plan works. Dollar everywhere. Domestic flights and TSA lines.

Bahamas: Independent nation. Valid U.S. passport required. Bahamian dollar is pegged 1:1 to USD (both accepted), but international phone plan needed. Inter-island flights can be expensive and infrequent.

Bottom line: The Bahamas is the most expensive destination in the Caribbean. Hotels, food, and activities run 30–50% higher than Puerto Rico. A week in PR: $1,200–1,800. A week in the Bahamas: $2,000–3,500. Puerto Rico has a genuine budget tier (guesthouses, food trucks, free beaches) that the Bahamas largely lacks.

Beaches

Bahamas: Among the clearest water anywhere on Earth. Pink Sands Beach on Harbour Island is bucket-list material. Cable Beach in Nassau, the Exumas with their sandbars and swimming pigs. Pristine but often resort-access or boat-access only.

Puerto Rico: Over 300 beaches across the main island and Culebra/Vieques. Flamenco Beach consistently ranks in the world’s top 5. More variety — white, golden, black sand, surf breaks, secluded coves. Most are free and publicly accessible.

Unique Experiences

Puerto Rico has:

  • Three permanent bioluminescent bays (Bahamas has zero)
  • El Yunque — the only tropical rainforest in the U.S. National Forest system
  • Old San Juan — 500 years of colonial forts and cobblestone streets
  • Island-hopping to Culebra and Vieques without a passport

Bahamas has:

  • Swimming with pigs at Big Major Cay in the Exumas
  • Pink Sands Beach on Harbour Island
  • Thunderball Grotto — the James Bond snorkeling cave
  • Dean’s Blue Hole — the world’s deepest known blue hole (663 feet)

Bio bays: only in Puerto Rico

The Bahamas has nothing comparable to Puerto Rico’s three permanent bioluminescent bays. Mosquito Bay on Vieques is one of the brightest in the world — paddle through water that lights up with every stroke. This is a once-in-a-lifetime experience you can only get in Puerto Rico. Safety

Puerto Rico: U.S. federal law enforcement, 911 service, FEMA disaster response, and U.S.-standard hospitals accepting most American insurance.

Bahamas: The State Department maintains a Level 2 advisory (exercise increased caution) due to crime, particularly in Nassau and Freeport. Resort islands and Out Islands are generally safer. Travel insurance recommended.

Getting Around

Puerto Rico: Compact main island — drive coast to coast in under 3 hours. Rental cars widely available, Uber works in San Juan. Ferries to Culebra and Vieques.

Bahamas: 700 islands spread across 100,000 square miles of ocean. Getting between islands requires flights or boat charters. Nassau is accessible but the best experiences (Exumas, Harbour Island) require extra travel and cost.

Which to Choose

Choose Puerto Rico if: Budget matters, no passport, bio bays, rainforest and history, family-friendly, want to explore independently.

Choose Bahamas if: You want the clearest water on Earth, pink-sand beaches, swimming with pigs, a luxury resort experience, or world-class diving.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a passport for Puerto Rico or the Bahamas? Puerto Rico: no — U.S. territory, fly with a driver’s license. The Bahamas requires a valid passport.

Is the Bahamas or Puerto Rico more expensive? The Bahamas is 30–50% more expensive for hotels, food, and activities. Puerto Rico has a genuine budget tier the Bahamas lacks.

Which has better beaches? The Bahamas has the clearest water and pink-sand beaches. Puerto Rico has 300+ beaches with more variety including surf, black sand, and secluded coves.

Does the Bahamas have bioluminescent bays? No. Puerto Rico has three permanent bio bays, including Mosquito Bay on Vieques — one of the brightest in the world.

Which is safer? Puerto Rico has U.S. law enforcement and healthcare. The State Department has a Level 2 advisory for the Bahamas due to crime in Nassau and Freeport.
