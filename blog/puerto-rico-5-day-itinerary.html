Puerto Rico 5-Day Itinerary: The Complete Trip (2026)

Five days is the sweet spot for Puerto Rico — enough time to cover the big highlights and still discover the island beyond San Juan. This itinerary takes you from colonial forts and bioluminescent bays to a tropical rainforest, an offshore island beach, and the laid-back west coast. No passport needed, domestic flights, and a trip that's hard to beat for value in the Caribbean.

Before You Go

  • Where to stay: Split your nights: 3 in San Juan (Old San Juan, Condado, or Isla Verde) and 2 on the west coast (Rincón or Aguadilla). Or stay in San Juan all 5 nights and day-trip.
  • Rental car: Recommended. San Juan is walkable, but El Yunque, Fajardo, and the west coast need wheels. Rentals run $40–$70/day.
  • Book ahead: Bio bay tours and Culebra ferry/tours sell out. Reserve at least a week in advance.
  • Budget: $1,200–$2,500 per person for 5 days (flights + hotels + food + car + tours).

Day 1: Old San Juan

Morning

Start at Castillo San Felipe del Morro (El Morro) — the six-level fortress on the headland that defines the San Juan skyline. Walk the ramparts, explore the tunnels, and watch locals flying kites on the lawn. Then head to Castillo San Cristóbal, the largest Spanish-built fortification in the Americas, with views across the city.

Afternoon

Walk the blue cobblestone streets. Calle del Cristo for galleries and shops, Calle Fortaleza for the famous umbrella street photo op. Lunch at a local spot — try mofongo (mashed plantains with garlic) and a piña colada at Barrachina, where the drink was invented. Walk the Paseo de la Princesa promenade along the waterfront.

Evening

Dinner in Old San Juan or Condado. If it's a Thursday–Saturday, head to La Placita in Santurce — the market square transforms into the best open-air nightlife block in the Caribbean. Live salsa, reggaetón, and cold drinks in the street.

Day 2: El Yunque & Bio Bay

Morning: El Yunque Rainforest

Early start to El Yunque National Forest (45 min from San Juan). Hit La Mina Falls — a 30-minute hike to a swimmable waterfall pool. If you have time, add Juan Diego Falls (less crowded) or Mount Britton Tower for panoramic views. Wear shoes that can get wet.

Afternoon: Luquillo Beach & Kiosks

Stop at Balneario de Luquillo (15 min from El Yunque) — calm water, palm-lined sand, and the Luquillo Kiosks across the road for alcapurrias, bacalaítos, fresh coconut, and cold beer. Best post-hike reward on the island.

Evening: Bioluminescent Bay

Continue to Fajardo for a night kayaking tour of Laguna Grande. Every paddle stroke lights up electric blue. This is the can't-miss experience of the trip — book well ahead.

Day 3: Offshore Island Day Trip

Pick one:

Option A: Culebra & Flamenco Beach

Take the ferry from Ceiba to Culebra (45 min) or book a catamaran day trip from Fajardo. Flamenco Beach is regularly ranked among the world's best — white sand, turquoise water, no development, no crowds on weekdays. Snorkel the reef at Tamarindo Beach on the way back. Full-day commitment but worth it.

Option B: Catamaran to Icacos or Palomino

Half-day catamaran from Fajardo to uninhabited island cays. Snorkeling, swimming, drinks on the boat, lunch included. Less logistics than Culebra, and you're back by early afternoon with time to explore or relax.

Day 4: West Coast — Rincón

Morning: Drive West

Drive from San Juan to Rincón (about 2.5 hours via Highway 22 and Route 2). Check into your west coast hotel or guesthouse. Rincón has a completely different vibe — laid-back surf town, stunning sunsets, smaller crowds.

Afternoon: Beach & Explore

Hit Domes Beach for surfing or Steps Beach for calm snorkeling and sea turtle sightings. Walk the Rincón Lighthouse for panoramic coastal views. If it's winter (Nov–Mar), you might spot humpback whales from shore.

Evening: Sunset & Dinner

Rincón has the best sunsets in Puerto Rico — the west coast faces directly into them. Watch from the lighthouse, Pools Beach, or a waterfront restaurant. Dinner at a local seafood spot — fresh mahi-mahi, red snapper, or lobster.

Day 5: Southern Coast or Chill

Pick your pace for the last day:

Option A: Cabo Rojo & Playa Sucia

Drive 45 minutes south to Cabo Rojo. Hike to the Los Morrillos Lighthouse for dramatic cliff views, then descend to Playa Sucia (La Playuela) — a secluded beach framed by cliffs. Stop at the Cabo Rojo Salt Flats — surreal pink-tinged flats that make great photos. This is Puerto Rico at its least touristy.

Option B: Slow Morning in Rincón

Sleep in, grab coffee at a local café, one last beach session at Steps or Sandy Beach, then drive back to San Juan for your flight. The drive back takes about 2.5 hours — plan accordingly.

Option C: Ponce (if heading east)

If driving back via the southern route, detour through Ponce — Puerto Rico's second city. The Plaza Las Delicias is beautifully restored, the Parque de Bombas firehouse is iconic, and the Ponce Museum of Art is the best on the island.

Budget Breakdown

  • Flights: $150–$350 roundtrip (domestic, East Coast)
  • Hotels (5 nights): $750–$1,250 (mid-range, split between SJ and west coast)
  • Rental car (5 days): $200–$350
  • Bio bay tour: $55–$75 per person
  • Culebra day trip or catamaran: $80–$150 per person
  • Food (5 days): $200–$350 per person
  • Total per person: $1,200–$2,500

Tips

  • Bio bay timing: Book around the new moon for the brightest glow. See our moon phase guide.
  • Culebra ferry: Book online as soon as tickets open — they sell out fast, especially weekends.
  • Gas: Fill up before leaving San Juan heading west. Stations get sparser outside the metro.
  • Reef-safe sunscreen: Required by law in Puerto Rico. Protect the coral.
  • Flight timing: Arrive early Day 1, depart late Day 5 to squeeze out maximum time.

FAQ

Is 5 days enough?

Yes — it's the sweet spot. You'll cover San Juan, bio bay, rainforest, an offshore island, and the west coast without rushing.

Do I need a rental car?

Recommended for 5 days. San Juan works without one, but the rest of the island needs wheels. $40–$70/day.

How much does it cost?

$1,200–$2,500 per person including flights, hotels, car, food, and tours.

Should I stay in one place or move?

Splitting 3 nights San Juan + 2 nights Rincón gives you two distinct experiences. All-San Juan with day trips also works.

What should I not miss?

Bio bay kayaking, Old San Juan, and El Yunque. Add Culebra/Flamenco Beach if logistics allow.
