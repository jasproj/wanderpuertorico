12 Things to Do in Fajardo, Puerto Rico

Updated August 2026 · 10-minute read

Fajardo sits at the far northeast corner of Puerto Rico, where the Atlantic meets the Caribbean and the mainland gives way to a scatter of sand cays and offshore islands. This quiet harbor town punches well above its weight: it's the launch point for Culebra, Vieques, and the best snorkeling reefs on the east coast, plus one of the planet's few bioluminescent bays glows just minutes from the marina.

Why Fajardo?

Fajardo is Puerto Rico's east coast gateway — roughly one hour from San Juan by car, home to the ferry terminal serving Vieques and Culebra, and within a 30-minute drive of El Yunque National Forest. If you want beaches, islands, and rainforest without changing hotels, Fajardo is your base.

Whether you're chasing glowing water, white-sand cays, or a quiet lunch spot on the waterfront, here are the twelve best things to do in and around Fajardo.

1. Kayak the Bioluminescent Bay at Laguna Grande

Night Activity 2-3 Hours Guided Tour

Laguna Grande is one of only a few bioluminescent bays left on Earth. Paddle a kayak through a narrow mangrove channel and into the open lagoon, where every stroke of your paddle lights up the water in electric blue-green. Microscopic dinoflagellates produce the glow — and the darker the night, the more dramatic the effect.

Book on a moonless or new-moon night for the strongest bioluminescence. Most outfitters depart from the Las Croabas area between 7:30 and 9:00 PM. Expect roughly 75 minutes on the water after a short safety briefing. Reservations are essential — tours sell out weeks ahead during peak season.

2. Spend the Day at Seven Seas Beach

Beach Half Day Family Friendly

Seven Seas is Fajardo's signature beach — a protected crescent of soft sand tucked inside the Las Cabezas de San Juan Nature Reserve. The water is calm, clear, and shallow near shore, making it ideal for families and casual snorkelers. A reef runs along the eastern edge where you'll spot parrotfish, sea fans, and the occasional sea turtle.

There are bathrooms, picnic shelters, and a small parking lot (arrive before 10 AM on weekends to guarantee a spot). Bring your own snorkel gear and a cooler — the nearest food vendors are a short drive away in Las Croabas.

3. Take the Ferry to Culebra Island

Day Trip Full Day Ferry Ride

Culebra is a 17-mile ferry ride from the Ceiba terminal (just south of Fajardo), and Flamenco Beach alone makes the trip worth it — it routinely lands on "best beaches in the world" lists. The horseshoe bay has powdery white sand, turquoise water, and a pair of rusted tanks left over from Navy exercises that now serve as the island's most photographed landmarks.

The first ferry leaves around 6:00 AM and the last return is mid-afternoon, so plan a full day. Buy tickets online in advance — walk-up spots are not guaranteed. On the island, rent a golf cart to explore Zoni Beach, Tamarindo Beach (excellent snorkeling), and the tiny town of Dewey for lunch.

4. Ferry to Vieques Island

Day Trip / Overnight Full Day Ferry Ride

Vieques is Culebra's larger, wilder sibling — a former Navy island now home to the Vieques National Wildlife Refuge, wild horses roaming the roads, and Mosquito Bay, the brightest bioluminescent bay in the world. Sun Bay Beach offers wide open sand, and the refuge's hidden beaches (Red Beach, Blue Beach) feel like private islands.

The ferry from Ceiba to Vieques runs about 30 minutes. An overnight stay lets you hit Mosquito Bay after dark, but a day trip works if you focus on the beaches and grab lunch in Esperanza or Isabel Segunda. Same advice: book ferry tickets early.

5. Catamaran Snorkeling Tour to Icacos and Palominito Cays

Snorkeling Half Day Boat Tour

A handful of uninhabited cays sit just off Fajardo's coast, and catamaran tours make them easy to reach. Icacos is the largest — a sandy spit surrounded by reef where visibility regularly tops 30 feet. Palominito is smaller and more secluded, often with no one else on the beach. Most tours include both stops, plus gear, lunch, and rum punch.

Half-day catamarans typically depart from the Marina Puerto del Rey or the Las Croabas docks between 9:00 and 10:00 AM and return by early afternoon. It's one of the most popular activities on the east coast, so book at least a few days ahead.

6. Explore Las Cabezas de San Juan Nature Reserve

Eco-Tour 2-3 Hours Guided Only

This 316-acre reserve occupies Fajardo's northeast headland and packs seven distinct ecosystems into a compact area — mangroves, dry forest, coral reefs, lagoons, sandy beach, rocky shore, and offshore cays. The guided trolley tour takes you through the ecosystems and ends at the restored 19th-century Fajardo Lighthouse (Las Cabezas lighthouse) with panoramic views of the offshore islands.

Access is by reservation only through the Para la Naturaleza conservation trust. Tours run Wednesday through Sunday, in English and Spanish. The reserve also borders Laguna Grande, so you can pair a daytime eco-tour with an evening bio bay kayak for a full Fajardo nature day.

7. Visit the El Conquistador Resort Area

Resort Flexible Views

El Conquistador sits on a 300-foot cliff overlooking the ocean and the island of Palomino. Even if you're not staying at the resort, the area is worth a visit for the clifftop views alone — the panorama stretches from Culebra to St. Thomas on a clear day. The resort runs a ferry to private Palomino Island, a manicured beach with water sports and a beachside grill.

The surrounding neighborhood has a few local restaurants and a boardwalk-style path with overlooks. Grab sunset drinks at one of the clifftop spots and watch the light change over the cays.

8. Kayak the Mangrove Channels

Kayaking 2 Hours Nature

Fajardo's coastline is laced with red mangrove channels — narrow, shaded waterways that wind through root systems teeming with juvenile fish, crabs, and wading birds. Daytime kayak tours take you through these tunnels at a relaxed pace, with guides pointing out herons, mangrove crabs, upside-down jellyfish, and the occasional manatee in deeper water.

This is the mellow counterpart to the bio bay tour — great for mornings, great for kids, and a solid option if you want to paddle without waiting for nightfall. Several outfitters launch from the same Las Croabas area used for the bio bay tours.

9. Deep-Sea Fishing from Puerto del Rey Marina

Fishing Half / Full Day Charter

Puerto del Rey is the largest marina in the Caribbean, and it's home base for Fajardo's deep-sea fishing fleet. Charters head out to the shelf drop-off where blue marlin, yellowfin tuna, wahoo, mahi-mahi, and sailfish patrol the deep water. The run to productive fishing grounds is short — often under 30 minutes — so you spend more time with lines in the water.

Half-day trips (4-5 hours) work well for first-timers; full-day charters push further offshore for the big pelagics. Most boats include tackle, bait, ice, and a cooler. Some captains will clean your catch so you can take it to a local restaurant for cooking — several in Las Croabas will prepare your fish for a small fee.

10. Luquillo Beach and the Famous Kiosks

Beach + Food Half Day 15 Min from Fajardo

Luquillo Beach sits about 15 minutes south of Fajardo and pairs a long, palm-lined strand with one of Puerto Rico's best food experiences: the Luquillo kiosks. Dozens of open-air stands line the road behind the beach, serving alcapurrias, empanadillas, fresh seafood, piña coladas, and every fried snack the island is known for.

The beach itself is wide, gentle, and swimmable, with a designated accessible section (Mar Sin Barreras). Combine a morning in Fajardo with an afternoon at Luquillo — swim, eat, repeat. Weekends are lively; weekdays are quieter but most kiosks still open by late morning.

11. Day Trip to El Yunque National Forest

Rainforest Half / Full Day 30 Min from Fajardo

El Yunque is the only tropical rainforest in the U.S. National Forest system, and its main entrance gate is about 30 minutes west of Fajardo on PR-3 and PR-191. Trails range from paved, wheelchair-accessible paths to rocky climbs up to the cloud forest at the summit. La Mina Falls is the marquee hike — a 0.7-mile trail to a 35-foot waterfall where you can swim in the pool below.

A reservation is required to enter by car (book through Recreation.gov). Arrive early — the forest opens at 7:30 AM and trails are least crowded before 10. Pair a morning hike with an afternoon at Seven Seas Beach or a sunset bio bay tour for one of the best day combos on the island.

12. Walk the Fajardo Lighthouse and Waterfront

Sightseeing 1-2 Hours Free

Fajardo's waterfront area, centered around the Las Croabas fishing village, is the town's social hub. Fishing boats line the docks, and a handful of no-frills seafood restaurants serve the catch of the day at plastic tables with water views. It's the real Puerto Rico — unpretentious, local, and excellent.

From here, you can walk or drive up to the headland where the Fajardo Lighthouse (part of the Las Cabezas reserve) sits on a bluff with views stretching to the Virgin Islands. The waterfront is also the staging area for most boat tours and kayak outfitters, so it's likely where your day begins and ends regardless.

Fajardo FAQ

Is the bioluminescent bay in Fajardo worth visiting?

Absolutely. Laguna Grande is one of a handful of bioluminescent bays left on Earth. Kayaking through glowing water on a moonless night is a bucket-list experience. Book a guided kayak tour departing after dark, and check the lunar calendar ahead of time — the darker the sky, the brighter the glow.

How do I get to Culebra or Vieques from Fajardo?

The Ceiba ferry terminal, just south of Fajardo, runs passenger ferries to both islands daily. The ride to Culebra takes about 45 minutes; Vieques is roughly 30 minutes. Tickets can be purchased online through the Puerto Rico ferry system. Book early — ferries sell out, especially on weekends and holidays.

How far is Fajardo from San Juan?

About 35 miles southeast — roughly a one-hour drive via PR-66 and PR-3. Fajardo makes a great day trip from San Juan or an overnight base for the east coast, El Yunque, and the offshore islands.

What is the best beach in Fajardo?

Seven Seas Beach is the top choice — a calm, crescent-shaped strand with clear snorkeling water and shaded picnic areas. For something more remote, take a catamaran to Icacos or Palominito cay, where you'll find pristine sand and excellent reef snorkeling with far fewer people.

Can I visit El Yunque and Fajardo in the same day?

Yes, and it's one of the island's best day-trip combos. El Yunque's main entrance is only 30 minutes west of Fajardo. A popular plan: hike El Yunque in the morning, grab lunch at the Luquillo kiosks, then head to Fajardo for a beach afternoon at Seven Seas or a sunset bio bay tour.
