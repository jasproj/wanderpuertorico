Puerto Rico Destination Wedding Guide: Planning, Venues & Legal Tips

Updated August 2026 · 9 min read

Puerto Rico is one of the best destination wedding locations in the Caribbean — and one of the easiest. No passports for US guests, no currency exchange, no language barrier, and marriage licenses are legally valid in all 50 states. Add world-class venues, tropical scenery, and prices 20–40% lower than comparable mainland destinations, and you've got a wedding that feels exotic without the logistics headache.

Why Puerto Rico for a Wedding

  • No passport required — US citizens fly in with just a driver's license. Huge for guest attendance.
  • Legally valid — marriages are recognized in all US states. No extra paperwork when you get home.
  • Direct flights — nonstop from most major US cities. JFK, MIA, ATL, ORD, DFW, LAX all have multiple daily flights.
  • Venue variety — beachfront resorts, colonial courtyards, mountain haciendas, private island villas, historic forts. More variety than any other Caribbean island.
  • Lower costs — 20–40% less than comparable mainland venues. Your dollar goes further on flowers, catering, and photography.
  • Built-in honeymoon — stay on the island after the wedding. See our honeymoon guide.

Legal Requirements

Marriage License

Apply at the Demographic Registry (CRIM office) in the municipality where the ceremony will take place. Both partners must appear in person with valid photo ID. The license costs about $20 and is valid for 10 days from issuance. You can apply 1–2 days before the wedding — no extended waiting period.

Blood Tests

Puerto Rico requires blood tests (VDRL — a syphilis screening) for both partners within 10 days of applying for the license. Many clinics on the island offer walk-in testing with same-day results for about $15–$25 per person. Your wedding planner can arrange this.

Officiant

The ceremony must be performed by a licensed officiant — a judge, notary public, or ordained minister authorized in Puerto Rico. Most wedding planners have preferred officiants. Bilingual (English/Spanish) officiants are common. Religious ceremonies in churches are also legally valid.

Documents Needed

Valid photo ID (passport or driver's license) for both partners, divorce decree if previously married, death certificate of former spouse if widowed, and parental consent if either partner is under 21. All documents must be in Spanish or accompanied by a certified translation.

Best Venue Types

Beachfront Resorts

The most popular choice — ceremony on the sand, reception under the stars. Resorts handle catering, décor, coordination, and guest rooms in one package. The St. Regis Bahia Beach (Río Grande), Condado Vanderbilt (San Juan), El Conquistador (Fajardo), and Dorado Beach (Ritz-Carlton Reserve) are the top tier. Mid-range options exist in Rincón and Cabo Rojo at half the price.

Budget: $15,000–$100,000+ depending on guest count and resort tier.

Historic Old San Juan

Colonial courtyards, rooftop terraces, and 500-year-old architecture as your backdrop. Hotel El Convento (a converted 17th-century convent) is the most iconic venue. Casa Blanca (the oldest European-built residence in the Americas) hosts private events. Several rooftop bars and restaurants offer semi-private reception space with harbor views.

Budget: $10,000–$50,000. Smaller spaces suit intimate weddings (under 50 guests) beautifully.

Private Villas

Rent a luxury villa in Vieques, Rincón, or Dorado and bring in your own vendors. This gives you total control over the vibe — a barefoot beach wedding, a pool party reception, a weekend-long celebration with your closest people. Villa rentals start around $500/night for a 4-bedroom; luxury estates run $2,000–$5,000/night.

Budget: $5,000–$30,000 (villa + vendors). Best for intimate weddings under 30 guests.

Mountain Haciendas

Coffee haciendas in the central mountains offer a dramatically different setting — lush green valleys, cooler temperatures, rustic-elegant charm. Hacienda Siesta Alegre near Río Grande and several haciendas in the Jayuya/Adjuntas area host weddings with a farm-to-table feel. Perfect for couples who want something beyond the beach.

Budget: $8,000–$40,000. Often includes outdoor ceremony space and reception hall.

When to Get Married

Best months: December–April

Low humidity, minimal rain, comfortable 78–85°F temperatures. January–March is peak wedding season — book venues 12–18 months ahead. December is beautiful but pricier due to holiday travel.

Shoulder season: May, June, November

Great weather with occasional afternoon showers (they pass quickly). 15–25% lower venue prices than peak season. November after hurricane season is especially smart — good weather, fewer crowds, grateful vendors.

Avoid: September–October

Peak hurricane season. Even without a storm, the risk means travel insurance complications, guest anxiety, and the possibility of a last-minute scramble. Not worth it.

Guest Experience

One of the biggest advantages of a Puerto Rico wedding is what your guests get to do before and after. Unlike a resort-only island, Puerto Rico offers a full vacation — Old San Juan sightseeing, rainforest hikes, bio bay kayaking, food tours, beach hopping, and nightlife. Many couples plan a welcome dinner and let guests explore the island on their own.

Budget Breakdown

  • Venue + catering: $5,000–$50,000 (the biggest variable — resort vs. villa vs. restaurant)
  • Photography + video: $2,500–$8,000
  • Flowers + décor: $1,500–$6,000
  • Officiant: $200–$500
  • Music/DJ: $800–$3,000
  • Wedding planner: $2,000–$8,000 (highly recommended for destination weddings)
  • Marriage license + blood tests: ~$100
  • Hair + makeup: $300–$800
  • Guest welcome bags: $15–$30/guest

Total range: $5,000–$15,000 for intimate (under 20 guests), $20,000–$50,000 for mid-size (50–80), $50,000–$150,000+ for luxury (100+).

Planning Tips

Hire a local planner

A Puerto Rico-based wedding planner is worth every dollar for a destination wedding. They know the vendors, handle the legal paperwork, manage day-of logistics, and solve problems you can't solve from the mainland. Budget $2,000–$8,000 depending on scope.

Visit before you book

A planning trip 8–12 months before the wedding lets you see venues in person, meet vendors, taste catering menus, and get a feel for the area. Many couples combine this with an engagement photo shoot on the island.

Hotel room blocks

Book a room block for guests 6–12 months ahead, especially for peak season. Most resorts offer group rates (10–20% off) and will hold rooms until 30–60 days before. Give guests 2–3 hotel options at different price points — not everyone wants the resort.

Travel insurance

Require it for hurricane-season weddings, recommend it for all. Event cancellation insurance covers vendor deposits and non-refundable costs. Guest travel insurance covers flights and hotel if a storm forces cancellation. This is not optional for summer weddings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it legal to get married in Puerto Rico?

Yes — Puerto Rico is a US territory, so marriages are legally valid in all 50 states. You need a marriage license, blood tests, and a licensed officiant. No passport required for US citizens.

How much does a destination wedding in Puerto Rico cost?

$5,000–$15,000 for intimate (under 20 guests), $20,000–$50,000 for mid-size, $50,000–$150,000+ for luxury. Generally 20–40% less than comparable mainland venues.

What is the best time of year?

December through April for the best weather. January–March is peak wedding season. November is the smart shoulder-season pick. Avoid September–October (hurricane season).

Do guests need a passport?

No — US citizens fly to Puerto Rico with just a driver's license or state ID. Same as any domestic US flight. This is a major advantage over other Caribbean destinations.

What are the best wedding venues?

Beachfront resorts (St. Regis, Condado Vanderbilt), historic Old San Juan venues (Hotel El Convento), private villas in Vieques or Rincón, and mountain haciendas. Puerto Rico has more venue variety than almost any other Caribbean island.
