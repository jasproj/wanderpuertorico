Ponce: Puerto Rico's Pearl of the South & Its Iconic Lions

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read Tours you can book here →

Puerto Rico's second-largest city has always felt like its own sovereign cultural capital. Ponce sits on the southern coast, two hours from San Juan on the other side of the central mountains, and the distance has allowed it to develop an identity entirely its own — one built on 19th-century wealth, distinctive architecture, civic pride, and an obsession with the lion that borders on the religious.

La Perla del Sur

The nickname La Perla del Sur — Pearl of the South — dates from Ponce's boom years in the 19th century, when the city grew rich from sugar, coffee, and rum exports. At its peak, Ponce was Puerto Rico's most commercially important city, with merchant families who built mansions in a distinctive local architectural style: creole neoclassical, with wide overhanging balconies, decorative columns, and vibrant facades painted in mustard, salmon, and ochre. The historic center today contains one of the finest concentrations of 19th-century Caribbean architecture on the island.

Parque de Bombas

The most photographed building in Ponce — possibly in all of southern Puerto Rico — is a firehouse. Parque de Bombas, built in 1882 as a temporary exhibition pavilion and then converted into the city's fire station, is a two-story structure painted in bold alternating stripes of red and black, with ornate Victorian detailing on the cornice. It stands at the south end of Plaza Las Delicias, the city's central square, and functions today as a small fire museum. The contrast between its exuberant exterior and its municipal purpose is so extreme that it stops people mid-stride.

The Lions

The lion has been the symbol of Ponce since the city received its coat of arms from the Spanish Crown. In the heraldic tradition, the lion signified nobility, strength, and civic virtue. Over centuries, the symbol took on a life of its own: lion sculptures appear on fountains, gates, and public buildings throughout the city. Plaza Las Delicias has lions guarding its fountain. The city's professional basketball team, the Leones de Ponce, carries the symbol forward into the sports world. For ponceños, the lion is not just a coat of arms detail — it is a declaration of who they are.

The Museum

The Museo de Arte de Ponce is one of the finest art museums in the Caribbean. It was established largely through the collection of Luis A. Ferré, a Puerto Rican industrialist and former governor, and holds significant holdings of European and Latin American art from the 14th century to the present. The building itself, designed by Edward Durell Stone and opened in 1965, is architecturally striking — a hexagonal structure with a distinctive honeycomb facade. The museum has periodically closed for renovation; check current hours when planning a visit.

Caja de Muertos

From Ponce's port, boat tours reach Caja de Muertos (Coffin Island), a small uninhabited island roughly nine miles offshore. The name comes from the island's silhouette, which from certain angles resembles a coffin. It has a lighthouse built in 1887, a small beach, and some of the clearest water on Puerto Rico's south coast for snorkeling. Day trips run on weekends when weather permits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Ponce called the Pearl of the South? Ponce earned the nickname for its wealth and cultural prominence during the 19th-century sugar and rum economy — Puerto Rico's commercial rival to San Juan.

What are the lions of Ponce? The lion is the heraldic symbol on Ponce's coat of arms, appearing throughout the city's public spaces and architecture. The basketball team Leones de Ponce also carries it.
