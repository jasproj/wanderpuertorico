Puerto Rico Packing List: What to Bring & What to Leave Home (2026)

Packing for Puerto Rico is easy if you know what actually matters. It's tropical, casual, and — since it's a U.S. territory — you can buy anything you forget at a Walgreens or Walmart on the island. That said, a few things are worth getting right before you go. Here's the complete packing list, organized by category, with specific tips for Puerto Rico (not generic tropical advice).

Clothing

Everyday Wear

  • Lightweight shorts (3-4 pairs) — quick-dry material is ideal
  • Tank tops, t-shirts, or linen shirts (4-5) — breathable fabrics only
  • Sundresses or casual dresses (2-3) — perfect for Old San Juan and dinner
  • One light long-sleeve layer — for air-conditioned restaurants and evening breezes
  • One pair of lightweight pants or a maxi skirt — some churches and upscale spots expect covered legs
  • Underwear and socks (pack extra — humidity means nothing dries fast)

Puerto Rico dress code: Casual everywhere. Even nice restaurants rarely require more than a clean shirt and closed-toe shoes. Leave the blazer, tie, and cocktail dress at home unless you're attending a specific event.

Swimwear

  • Swimsuits (2-3) — you'll wear one almost every day, and wet suits in a bag are miserable
  • Cover-up or sarong — for walking from the beach to a restaurant
  • Rash guard — useful for extended snorkeling (sun protection + jellyfish brush)

Rain & Layers

  • Packable rain jacket — non-negotiable. Tropical showers hit daily, especially in El Yunque and afternoon hours. More practical than an umbrella when your hands are full.
  • Light hoodie or sweater — air conditioning in Puerto Rico is aggressive. Restaurants, malls, and rental cars can feel arctic compared to outside.

Footwear

Three Pairs Cover Everything

  • Comfortable walking shoes with grip — Old San Juan's cobblestones are beautiful and treacherous when wet. Sneakers with non-slip soles or supportive sandals with traction. You'll walk 5-8 miles on a full sightseeing day.
  • Water shoes or sport sandals — for rocky beach entries, bio bay kayaking, El Yunque creek crossings, and snorkeling boat ladders. Keens, Chacos, or basic water shoes all work. This is the pair most people forget and most regret skipping.
  • Flip-flops — pool, beach, hotel room. Not for walking Old San Juan (blisters guaranteed on cobblestones).

Skip the hiking boots. El Yunque's trails are well-maintained — trail runners or even sturdy sneakers with grip are fine. Boots are overkill and take up luggage space. The trails are wet and sometimes muddy, so shoes you don't mind getting dirty are the move.

Sun & Skin Protection

Non-Negotiables

  • Reef-safe sunscreen (SPF 50+) — Puerto Rico beaches and marine reserves require reef-safe formulas. Look for "no oxybenzone, no octinoxate" on the label. Bring a full bottle — island prices are 40-60% higher than mainland.
  • Insect repellent with DEET or picaridin — mosquitoes are real, especially near mangroves (bio bay areas), the rainforest, and at dusk. Apply before every evening activity.
  • Aloe vera gel — you'll get more sun than you think. A small bottle saves a pharmacy trip.
  • Wide-brim hat or baseball cap — essential for beach days and the Playa Sucia / Cabo Rojo cliffs where there's zero shade.
  • Polarized sunglasses — the Caribbean glare off turquoise water is intense.

Beach & Water Gear

Bring These

  • Dry bag (10-20L) — keeps your phone, wallet, and keys safe on boat tours, kayak trips, and beach days. Cheap insurance for expensive electronics. Essential for bio bay kayaking.
  • Waterproof phone pouch — for bio bay photos (your phone's "water resistance" isn't rated for salt water) and boat spray. LifeProof or a cheap universal pouch both work.
  • Microfiber travel towel — dries fast, packs small. Some guesthouses and Airbnbs don't provide beach towels.
  • Reusable water bottle — tap water is safe to drink in Puerto Rico (it's regulated by the EPA, same as the mainland). Refill instead of buying plastic.

Optional (Skip If Light Packing)

  • Snorkel mask — tours provide gear, and rental is $10-$15 at beach shops. Only worth bringing your own if you have a prescription mask or snorkel frequently.
  • GoPro or underwater camera — if you already own one, bring it. Renting or buying on-island is expensive.
  • Beach umbrella — skip it. Most beaches have palm shade, and the logistics of flying with an umbrella aren't worth it.

Electronics & Tech

What to Bring

  • Phone + charger — your U.S. plan works (no roaming). Coverage is good in cities; spotty in mountains, Vieques interior, and wildlife refuges.
  • Portable charger / power bank — long days exploring drain batteries fast, especially with GPS navigation and photos. A 10,000mAh bank gets you through a full day.
  • Car phone mount — if you're renting a car, Google Maps is your best navigation tool. A $10 mount beats trying to follow audio directions on unfamiliar roads.
  • Headlamp or small flashlight — useful for bio bay tours (guides sometimes request red light only), El Yunque early mornings, and Vieques wildlife refuge roads at night.

No adapter needed. Puerto Rico uses the same outlets and voltage as the mainland U.S. (Type A/B, 120V). Bring your normal chargers.

Documents & Money

Essentials

  • Photo ID — a driver's license works. No passport required (Puerto Rico is a U.S. territory).
  • Credit/debit card — accepted almost everywhere. Visa and Mastercard have the widest acceptance.
  • Cash ($100-$150) — kioskos, lechoneras, small-town restaurants, and some parking lots are cash-only. ATMs are available at banks and Walgreens island-wide.
  • Health insurance card — your U.S. health insurance works in Puerto Rico. Bring your card just in case.
  • Tour confirmation emails — screenshot or print your bio bay, snorkeling, and other tour confirmations. Cell service can be unreliable at check-in locations.

What NOT to Pack

  • Formal clothing — Puerto Rico is casual. Even nice restaurants don't require jackets or heels.
  • Heavy hiking boots — overkill for El Yunque. Trail runners or grippy sneakers are better and lighter.
  • A passport — you don't need one. Save the worry.
  • A hair dryer — every hotel has one, and the humidity will undo your work in minutes anyway.
  • Non-reef-safe sunscreen — it's restricted at protected beaches. Buy the right kind before you go (cheaper on the mainland).
  • Expensive jewelry — leave it home. Beach sand, salt water, and adventure activities are hard on valuables.
  • More than one week's clothing — laundromats and hotel laundry services are available everywhere. Pack light, wash once.

Packing for Specific Activities

Bio Bay Kayaking

Wear: dark clothing (guides request it to reduce light interference), water shoes, swimsuit underneath. Bring: dry bag, waterproof phone pouch, insect repellent (apply before arrival — no aerosol sprays near the bay). Leave: flashlights with white light (red light only, or guide-provided).

El Yunque Hiking

Wear: quick-dry shorts and shirt, trail runners or grippy sneakers, rain jacket. Bring: water bottle, snacks, insect repellent, change of dry clothes for the car. Leave: cotton clothing (stays wet for hours), jeans, flip-flops.

Snorkeling / Boat Tours

Wear: swimsuit, rash guard for sun protection. Bring: reef-safe sunscreen (reapply after swimming), waterproof phone pouch, dry bag, towel, sunglasses with a strap. Leave: valuables on shore (lockers available at some beaches).

Old San Juan Walking

Wear: comfortable walking shoes with grip (cobblestones), breathable clothing, hat. Bring: water bottle, camera, light layer for air-conditioned stops. Leave: flip-flops (cobblestones + hills = blisters), heavy bags.
