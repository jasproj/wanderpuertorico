Best Time to Visit Puerto Rico: Weather, Seasons & Hurricane Guide

Updated April 2026 • 8 min read

Puerto Rico has year-round warm weather, but season dramatically affects your experience. Understand temperature, rainfall, hurricanes, and prices before booking.

The Four Seasons

🌞 Dry Season (May–November)

Best for: Budget travelers, fewer crowds, lower prices

Weather: Warm, occasional showers, calm ocean mornings

Prices: 20–40% cheaper than winter

Downside: Hurricane risk (June–Nov, peak August–September)

❄️ Winter/High Season (December–April)

Best for: Perfect weather, cruising, beach time

Weather: 75–85°F, dry, sunny, low humidity

Crowds: Maximum (holiday + spring break)

Prices: 30–50% premium vs summer

Hurricane Season (June–November)

⚠️ Hurricane Risk Timeline
  • June–July: Lowest risk (~5% direct hit)
  • August–September: Peak hurricane season (~20% risk of impact)
  • October–November: Moderate risk (~10–15%)

What to know: Even during peak season, direct hits are rare. Most years pass without one. Insurance is cheap ($5–$10/day). Weather windows often allow tours/activities even during season.

Best Time for Specific Activities

🌊 Snorkeling & Water Sports: May–November (calm mornings, warm water)

🐋 Whale Watching: January–March (peak migration, visible offshore)

🌅 Weather Photography: January–April (clear, dramatic light)

💰 B

🏖️ Crowds: Avoid December–April if solitude matters

My Recommendation

Sweet Spot: Late April–May or November

  • ✅ Good weather (still dry season)
  • ✅ Lower prices (not peak)
  • ✅ Smaller crowds
  • ✅ Ocean warm and calm
  • ✅ Low hurricane risk

Timing your trip for the bio-bay

One timing factor most Puerto Rico guides leave out: the moon phase. Bioluminescent bays glow brightest on dark nights around the new moon, when there's little moonlight to wash the effect out. A full moon can flatten the glow almost completely, so the calendar date matters less than where the moon is that night.
