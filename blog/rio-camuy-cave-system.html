Río Camuy Cave System: One of the Americas' Great Underground Rivers

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read Tours you can book here →

Under the green karst hills of Puerto Rico's northwest interior flows the Río Camuy — a river that disappears underground and travels for miles through one of the most extensive cave systems in the Western Hemisphere. The Río Camuy cave network contains more than 200 mapped caves, passages, and sinkholes, carved over millions of years by slightly acidic rainwater dissolving the region's limestone bedrock.

The Geology Behind the Caves

Puerto Rico's north coast sits on a thick belt of Oligocene and Miocene limestone — rock laid down as ancient seafloor and then uplifted. Over the past few million years, rainwater lightly acidified by dissolved carbon dioxide has slowly eaten through cracks in this limestone, dissolving the rock and creating tunnels, caverns, and underground rivers. The result is a karst landscape: from above, it looks like a maze of rounded green hills called mogotes, separated by depressions and sinkholes. Beneath it is a labyrinth.

The Río Camuy is not a small stream. It is a substantial river — one of the largest in Puerto Rico by discharge — and for much of its length it flows in complete darkness through passages large enough to walk or even boat through. It eventually re-emerges at the surface near the coast before reaching the sea.

What Visitors See

Parque de las Cavernas del Río Camuy, operated by Puerto Rico Parks and Recreation, provides guided access to three of the cave chambers. The centerpiece is Clara Cave (Cueva Clara de Empalme), a massive chamber that can reach heights of around 170 feet and widths of roughly 700 feet in places — large enough to hold an entire city block. Stalactites hang from the ceiling in formations built up over thousands of years. The underground river itself passes through sections of the cave system, audible even when not directly visible.

Tours follow paved walkways and use tram transport to move visitors between sinkholes. The humidity inside hovers around 100 percent and the temperature stays relatively constant year-round — a noticeable shift from the warm surface. The cave also hosts a large population of Puerto Rican bats, several species of which roost in the passages and emerge at dusk to feed on insects.

Scale and Context

Río Camuy is regularly listed among the largest underground cave systems in the Americas. The network dwarfs most cave parks in the United States and rivals the great cave systems of Central America in terms of extent and geological significance. It is also significantly less visited than famous cave parks in the continental US, which means a typical touring day involves small groups and a relatively uncrowded experience.

Planning a Visit

The park is located near the town of Camuy, roughly 90 minutes west of San Juan. Hours and capacity have varied due to staffing and maintenance — check current schedules with Puerto Rico's Parks and Recreation office before planning a day around it. The cave temperature is a constant relief from surface heat, but the humidity and uneven surfaces make sensible footwear essential.

Frequently Asked Questions

How large is the Río Camuy cave system? It contains over 200 mapped caves connected by the Río Camuy underground river, one of the largest underground river systems in the Western Hemisphere.

Is Río Camuy Cave Park open to visitors? Parque de las Cavernas del Río Camuy operates as a Puerto Rico Parks facility — check current hours before visiting as schedules vary.
