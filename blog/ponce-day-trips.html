Ponce Day Trips & Southwest Puerto Rico Adventures

Updated March 2026 · 9 min read

Ponce is Puerto Rico's second city, sitting on the south coast about 75 miles from San Juan via the expressway. The city itself has a well-preserved historic center, a legitimate art museum (the Museo de Arte de Ponce has one of the best pre-Raphaelite painting collections in the Western Hemisphere), and a food and nightlife scene that's completely independent of the tourist circuit that runs through San Juan, El Yunque, and Fajardo.

For outdoor activities, Ponce's main day trip draws on the surrounding southwest coast, particularly La Parguera—a mangrove-lined fishing village about 25 miles west of Ponce in the municipality of Lajas. La Parguera has one of Puerto Rico's three bioluminescent bays, a series of mangrove cays accessible by water taxi, and several boat tour operators who run snorkeling, bio bay, and sunset excursions into the bay system. This guide covers what's bookable from Ponce and the surrounding southwest coast.

La Parguera Bio Bay Tours

La Parguera's bioluminescent bay is the only one of Puerto Rico's three bio bays where swimming is still permitted. The bay isn't as bright as Mosquito Bay in Vieques (the world record holder), but the ability to get in the water and watch your body light up blue makes it a different kind of experience than the kayak-only tours at Fajardo and Vieques. Tours typically run in the evening and combine a sunset boat ride with the bio bay swim.

Sunset + Swim the Bioluminescent Bay Tour (Hook'd on Adventure Parguera)

Hook'd on Adventure Parguera runs one of La Parguera's most popular bio bay experiences: a combined sunset boat ride and bioluminescent bay swim. The tour departs in the late afternoon, takes in the sunset from the bay, and then waits for full dark before anchoring in the bio bay for the swim. Guests get in the water in an area of high dinoflagellate concentration and can watch their movements light up the surrounding water. The operator also includes snorkeling stops before the bio bay portion on some departure schedules.

Ponce's Own Attractions

Beyond the La Parguera water tours, Ponce offers several worthwhile on-land attractions that make it worth spending a night rather than just passing through:

  • Plaza Las Delicias: The central plaza with the famous black-and-red Cathedral of Our Lady of Guadalupe and the Parque de Bombas—a distinctive red-and-black striped firehouse that's become one of Puerto Rico's most photographed buildings.
  • Museo de Arte de Ponce: Serious art museum funded by the Ferré family with an impressive collection of European and Puerto Rican art. The pre-Raphaelite painting collection is exceptional by any standard.
  • Tibes Indigenous Ceremonial Center: Archaeological site north of the city with the oldest known ball courts in the Caribbean, dating to 600 AD. One of the most significant pre-Taíno sites in Puerto Rico.
  • Hacienda Buena Vista: A restored 19th-century coffee and corn hacienda north of Ponce, operated by the Conservation Trust of Puerto Rico. Tours run on weekends and show the water-powered mill that processed coffee for export.
Getting to La Parguera from Ponce: La Parguera is about 25 miles west of Ponce via PR-2 west to PR-116 south. The drive takes 30–40 minutes without traffic. La Parguera village has a parking area near the waterfront. The town fills on weekend evenings when locals from Ponce and San Juan come for the bio bay—arrive by early afternoon if you want to explore the village before your evening tour.

Southwest Puerto Rico: What Else Is Near Ponce

The south coast between Ponce and Lajas has several natural areas worth knowing about. Guánica State Forest is one of the world's best-preserved dry tropical forests—the opposite ecological pole from El Yunque. Its trails through cactus and dry scrub lead to ocean cliffs and snorkeling areas at Playa Santa. The Gilligan's Island chain of mangrove cays near Guánica requires a water taxi from the boat launch.

The Cabo Rojo lighthouse at the southwestern tip of Puerto Rico sits above sea cliffs with views of the offshore shoals. Salt flats at Cabo Rojo are an important migratory bird habitat—birders should build in time here. The adjacent beaches at Buye and Buyé are among the west coast's better-kept secrets.
