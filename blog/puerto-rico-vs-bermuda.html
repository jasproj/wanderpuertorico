Puerto Rico vs Bermuda: Costs, Beaches & Which to Pick in 2026

Both are popular with East Coast travelers looking for a quick island getaway — Puerto Rico is about a 3-hour flight, Bermuda about 2 hours from New York. But they deliver very different trips. Puerto Rico is a U.S. territory with tropical rainforests, bioluminescent bays, and budget-friendly options. Bermuda is a British territory with pink-sand beaches, manicured elegance, and prices to match. Here's the breakdown.

Passport & Logistics

Puerto Rico requires no passport — it's a U.S. territory, so you fly domestic with a driver's license, use your cell plan, and pay in U.S. dollars. Bermuda requires a valid U.S. passport. Bermuda uses the Bermudian dollar (pegged 1:1 with USD, and U.S. dollars are accepted everywhere). One major difference: Bermuda does not allow rental cars. You'll get around by scooter, taxi, bus, or electric rental car (Twizys). Puerto Rico works like any U.S. state for logistics.

Bermuda is one of the most expensive island destinations in the world. Hotels regularly run $400–$600/night for mid-range, and a casual lunch for two can easily hit $60–$80. There is no budget tier — no hostels, no $80/night guesthouses. Puerto Rico offers the full range from budget to luxury.

Beaches

Bermuda's Strengths

Bermuda's beaches are genuinely unique — the sand is pink, colored by crushed coral and shells mixed with fine white sand. Horseshoe Bay is the most famous, consistently ranked among the world's best. The water is clear and calm, and the beaches are well-maintained. The entire south shore is a string of beautiful coves connected by trails.

Puerto Rico's Strengths

Puerto Rico has more variety: Flamenco Beach in Culebra matches any beach in the hemisphere. Rincón has real surf. The southern coast has secluded coves. And Puerto Rico offers something Bermuda simply can't — three bioluminescent bays, including the brightest on Earth at Mosquito Bay, Vieques.

Unique Experiences

Only in Bermuda

  • Pink-sand beaches: The signature draw — you won't find naturally pink sand in Puerto Rico or most of the Caribbean.
  • Crystal Caves: Underground caverns with crystal-clear pools and dramatic stalactites. One of Bermuda's best non-beach attractions.
  • Shipwreck diving: Bermuda sits on a reef system with hundreds of shipwrecks. The diving and snorkeling on wrecks is world-class.
  • British colonial charm: Pastel-colored buildings, afternoon tea, cricket on the green, and the Royal Naval Dockyard. A distinctly different vibe from anywhere in the Caribbean.

Only in Puerto Rico

  • Bioluminescent bays: Three glowing bays, including Mosquito Bay in Vieques — the brightest in the world. Bermuda has nothing comparable.
  • El Yunque rainforest: The only tropical rainforest in the U.S. National Forest System, with waterfalls and hiking trails 30 minutes from San Juan.
  • Old San Juan: 500 years of Spanish colonial architecture, two massive forts, cobblestone streets, and a food scene with real depth.
  • Year-round warmth: Puerto Rico is beach-ready 365 days a year. Bermuda's winters (60–70°F) are too cool for most swimmers.

Food & Nightlife

Puerto Rico wins on both. Old San Juan and Santurce have a genuine restaurant scene from street food to chef-driven dining. Piña coladas were invented here, and La Placita's weekend nightlife is legendary. Puerto Rico has salsa, reggaetón, and a bar scene that runs late.

Bermuda's dining is refined but expensive — fresh fish, afternoon tea at hotel terraces, the Dark 'n' Stormy (the national drink). Nightlife is quiet by comparison. If food and going out are priorities, Puerto Rico delivers more for less.

Weather & Timing

This matters more than most travelers realize. Puerto Rico is tropical — warm year-round, 80–90°F, with a rainy season (August–November) that mostly means brief afternoon showers. Bermuda is subtropical — warm enough for beach days from May through October, but winter temperatures drop to 60–70°F, too cool for comfortable swimming. If you're booking a winter trip, Puerto Rico is the clear choice.

Safety & Healthcare

Both are safe for tourists. Bermuda has very low crime and a small-island feel. Puerto Rico is safe in tourist areas with standard awareness needed in metro San Juan. Puerto Rico has the healthcare advantage: U.S. hospitals, U.S. insurance accepted, 911. Bermuda has a good hospital (King Edward VII) but limited capacity for serious emergencies, and you'll need travel insurance.

Which Should You Pick?

Pick Bermuda if:

  • Pink-sand beaches and British colonial charm are the draw
  • Budget is not a primary concern
  • You want a refined, quieter island experience
  • Shipwreck diving is on your list
  • You're traveling May–October

Pick Puerto Rico if:

  • You want to skip the passport and fly domestic
  • Bioluminescent bays, rainforests, and variety matter
  • Budget matters — Puerto Rico delivers more for half the cost
  • Food and nightlife are priorities
  • You're traveling in winter (Bermuda is too cool for beaches)
  • U.S. healthcare and cell service are important

FAQ

Do I need a passport for Puerto Rico or Bermuda?

Puerto Rico: no — fly domestic. Bermuda: yes, a valid U.S. passport is required.

Is Bermuda or Puerto Rico more expensive?

Bermuda is 50–100% more expensive. A 5-night trip for two easily runs $4,500–$8,000 vs $2,200–$3,800 in Puerto Rico.

Which has better beaches?

Bermuda has iconic pink sand. Puerto Rico has more variety — surf, coves, offshore islands, and bioluminescent bays.

Does Bermuda have bioluminescent bays?

No. Puerto Rico has three, including the brightest on Earth.

When is the best time to visit each?

Puerto Rico works year-round. Bermuda is best May–October; winters are too cool for beach days.
