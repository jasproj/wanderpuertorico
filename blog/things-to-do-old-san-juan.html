18 Best Things to Do in Old San Juan, Puerto Rico

Updated October 2026 · 11-minute read Tours you can book here →

Old San Juan is a seven-block-wide islet at the mouth of San Juan Bay, walled in by Spain starting in 1539 and still almost entirely intact: two forts, three miles of wall, 400 restored buildings and the blue-grey cobblestones that came over as ship ballast. It is a 15-minute ride from the Condado hotels and the cruise ships tie up at its front door, which is why mornings are quiet and early afternoons are not.

This is the walkable list: everything below is within a mile of Plaza de Armas, and the tours are the ones you can book direct through the buttons. For the wider city see 25 Best Things to Do in San Juan.

The forts and the walls

1. Castillo San Felipe del Morro

Fort2 hours$10, includes San Cristóbal

The six-level citadel on the point, begun in 1539 and finished in 1787, which held off Drake in 1595 and the Dutch in 1625. Walk down to the water battery, up to the lighthouse and out along the garitas (sentry boxes) that are on the Puerto Rico license plate. The lawn in front is the kite field. The $10 ticket is good for 24 hours at both forts; US park passes work.

2. Castillo San Cristóbal

Fort1.5 hoursIncluded with El Morro

The bigger fort, built 1634–1790 to defend the land side, covering 27 acres with tunnels, a dungeon with sailors' ship drawings on the walls, and the best view back over the old city. Fewer people than El Morro. The Devil's Sentry Box at the sea end has its own legend.

3. Paseo del Morro and the San Juan Gate

Walk1 hourFree

A paved path that runs outside the city wall from the Gate (the 1635 red door that was the official entrance from the harbor) around the base of El Morro. Pelicans, iguanas, cats, and the wall above you. Cooler in the morning; it dead-ends at the fort so you come back the same way.

4. La Perla

Neighborhood30–60 minutesFree

The pastel settlement outside the wall between the forts, built in the 19th century and famous from the 'Despacito' video. It is a real neighborhood: walk the main lane, buy a drink at the beach bar, and be respectful with cameras. The stairs down are beside the cemetery.

5. Santa María Magdalena de Pazzis Cemetery

Landmark20 minutesFree, view from above

The whitewashed cemetery wedged between the wall and the sea below El Morro, with the red-domed chapel from 1863. Most visitors see it from the fort's lawn; it opens to visitors during the day via the tunnel by La Perla.

Streets and squares

6. Calle Fortaleza and the umbrellas

Street30 minutesFree

The main shopping street ending at La Fortaleza, the 1533 governor's mansion that is the oldest executive residence in continuous use in the Americas. The umbrella and flag installations over the last block change through the year and draw the photo crowds; go before 9am to get the street empty.

7. Calle del Cristo and Capilla del Cristo

Street45 minutesFree

The blue-cobbled street from the cathedral down to the tiny Capilla del Cristo (1753) at the dead end over the wall, built where a rider supposedly survived going over the cliff. Galleries, the Puerto Rican Crafts shop and Pigeon Park (Parque de las Palomas) beside the chapel.

8. Catedral de San Juan Bautista

Church30 minutesFree

The 1540 cathedral, second-oldest in the Americas, holding Ponce de León's tomb and the wax-covered relics of St. Pius. Open daily; services on Sundays. Plaza across the street is the Hotel El Convento, a 1651 Carmelite convent, whose courtyard bar is the right place for a drink afterwards.

9. Plaza de Armas and Plaza Colón

Squares30 minutesFree

Armas is the original main square with City Hall, the four seasons statues and the kiosk coffee; Colón is the eastern entrance with the 1893 Columbus statue and the Tapia Theater (1832), the oldest theater in Puerto Rico still in use. Both have shade and benches.

10. Casa Blanca

Museum1 hour$5

The 1521 house built for Ponce de León (he died before moving in) and lived in by his descendants for 250 years. Period rooms, a shaded garden with a fountain, and the quietest spot in the old city. Closed Mondays.

13. La Factoría

BarEveningDrinks $12–$16

A 2013 bar in a 19th-century building at Calle San Sebastián and San José, on the World's 50 Best Bars list for years: five rooms with their own bars, a dance floor at the back, no sign on the door. Opens late afternoon; the back rooms fill after 10pm.

14. Calle San Sebastián

NightlifeEveningFree

The bar street behind the Plaza de Armas and the site of the Fiestas de la Calle San Sebastián every January, the biggest street festival in the Caribbean. Nono's, El Batey (a dive with dollar bills on the ceiling), and a dozen others. Thursday to Saturday is the week.

15. Piña colada at Barrachina

Drink20 minutesAbout $12

The courtyard restaurant on Calle Fortaleza that claims the drink's 1963 invention (the Caribe Hilton in Condado claims 1954). Both make a good one. Barrachina's is the one inside the old city.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much time do you need in Old San Juan?

A full day covers both forts, the main streets, the cathedral and a meal. Half a day works if you skip San Cristóbal. Cruise passengers have about six hours and should start at El Morro before 10am.

How much does it cost to visit El Morro?

$10 per adult, free for children 15 and under, and the ticket covers both El Morro and San Cristóbal for 24 hours. US National Park passes are accepted. The forts open daily at 9am.

Is Old San Juan walkable?

Yes, it is seven blocks wide and everything is within a mile. The streets are steep and cobbled, so wear real shoes. A free trolley loops the old city, and the forts are a 15-minute walk apart along the wall.

Are guided tours of Old San Juan worth it?

For a first visit, yes: a two-hour walk runs $25–$65 per person and explains the forts, the wall and the streets in a way the signs do not. Food tours at about $139 add five or six tastings and double as lunch.
