Scuba Diving in Puerto Rico: Beginner Dives, Boat Trips & Certification

Updated August 2026 · 6 min read

Puerto Rico is one of the few places where you can be underwater on a real dive within an hour of landing — no boat required. San Juan has a shore dive minutes from the hotel strip, the west coast around Rincón and Aguadilla runs guided shore dives morning and night, and the east side launches boat trips to the reefs off Fajardo, Culebra, and Vieques. Never held a regulator in your life? Several operators here will have you breathing underwater the same day, no certification needed. This guide covers every dive currently bookable on the island — with real prices — sorted by the only question that matters: are you certified, or not yet?

The Try Scuba Diving Tour in San Juan is the easy on-ramp: $89, ages 10 and up (youth and adult price the same), 9:00 AM start. If you're staying in San Juan it's the least logistics of any dive on the island.

Also running: the Beginner Scuba Diving Experience with SCUBA DVNG at $95 per person (about an hour, 9:00 AM), and 413 Divers' Beginner Scuba Diving Tour at $125 for adults and kids 10–17 alike, 8:00 AM start. Want the instructor all to yourself? Crashboat Watersports runs a Private Discover Scuba Diver Adventure in Aguadilla — $175 for a private four-hour session.

Not ready to breathe through a regulator? Sea Trek with Aqua Adventures is the no-swim version — a helmet walk on the sea floor, $103 for anyone 3 and up, about 90 minutes.

Certified? Your Card Works Hard Here

Shore dives, west coast. Rincon Diving & Snorkeling's Guided Shore Dive is the value play of the island: $79 for one tank, $109 for two — and bring a buddy, because two or more divers drop it to $69 and $99. Morning start at 8:30. In Aguadilla, Crashboat's Private Guided Shore Dive is a two-tank private trip at $150.

Shore dive, San Juan. Aqua Adventures runs Dive San Juan at the Caribe Hilton — a one-tank beach dive at $88.79 with all gear included, 10:00 AM. Closest dive to the cruise port and Condado.

Boat trips, east coast. Sea Ventures Pro Dive Center runs the Fajardo Reefs Morning Dive Excursion — certified divers pay $130, and the boat also takes snorkelers at $99 and ride-alongs, so mixed groups work (7:45 AM, about 4 hours). Their Culebra Reef Dive Excursion runs certified divers at $155 (snorkelers $145, kids $125) on select dates, and the Vieques 2 Tank Dive Trip at $155 runs a limited calendar — both are check-the-calendar trips, not book-tonight trips.

Rusty? Take a Refresher

If it's been years since your last dive, a half-day refresher beats white-knuckling a boat trip: Crashboat's ReActivate Scuba Refresher Course is $139 (4 hours, 8:00 AM), and Rincon Diving's PADI Scuba Refresher: Dive Back In is $169 (4 hours, 8:30 AM).

Get Certified in Puerto Rico

Both west-coast shops teach the full PADI Open Water Scuba Diver course — 5 confined-water sessions and 4 open-water dives — at $600, in Rincón (Rincon Diving & Snorkeling) or Aguadilla (Crashboat Watersports). Rincón sweetens it for pairs: two or more students enroll at $550 each, and they also offer the shorter Scuba Diver certification (3 confined sessions, 2 open-water dives) at $450. Doing the course on vacation means your certification dives happen on Caribbean reef instead of a quarry back home.

Which Dive Should You Book?

First time underwater: the $89 San Juan try-dive if you're in the capital, the $95 beginner experience otherwise. Certified and budget-minded: Rincón's $79 shore dive — $69 each with a buddy — is the best cost-per-tank on the island. Want a boat day: Fajardo at $130 certified, with snorkeling seats at $99 for the non-divers in your group. For something you can't do at home: the $139 twilight two-tank. And if you've always meant to get certified, $600 on a Caribbean coastline is the way to finally do it.

Pro Tips for Diving Puerto Rico

  1. Book morning dives early in your trip. Almost everything here departs 7:45–10:00 AM. Front-load the diving, keep the afternoons.
  2. Bring your certification card. Boat operators price certified divers and snorkelers differently — you'll be asked.
  3. Buddy up for the west coast. Rincón's shore dives and night dives all drop the per-person price at two or more divers.
  4. The island trips need calendar flexibility. Culebra and Vieques boat dives run select dates — check availability first and build the rest of your itinerary around them.
  5. No-fly window. Don't dive within 18–24 hours of your flight home — schedule the last tank accordingly.

FAQ

How much does scuba diving cost in Puerto Rico?

Guided shore dives start at $79 for certified divers ($69 each for two or more). First-time beginner dives run $89–$125 with no certification needed. Boat trips to the Fajardo reefs are $130 for certified divers, and the full PADI Open Water certification course is $600.

Can I scuba dive in Puerto Rico without being certified?

Yes — instructor-led beginner dives run daily from $89, ages 10 and up, with all instruction included. There's also a $103 helmet-walk option (Sea Trek) for ages 3+ that doesn't require swimming.

Where is the best scuba diving in Puerto Rico?

It depends on your base: San Juan has an $88.79 shore dive with gear included, the west coast (Rincón/Aguadilla) has the cheapest tanks and the night dives, and the east coast runs boat trips to Fajardo, Culebra, and Vieques reefs.

Can I get scuba certified in Puerto Rico?

Yes — two west-coast shops teach the full PADI Open Water course at $600 ($550 each for two or more students in Rincón), typically built around 5 confined-water sessions and 4 open-water dives.
