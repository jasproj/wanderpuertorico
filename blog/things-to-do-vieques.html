12 Things to Do in Vieques, Puerto Rico

Updated August 2026 · 10-minute read

Vieques is a 21-mile island off Puerto Rico's east coast that runs on its own clock. No chain hotels, no traffic lights, no cruise ships. Two-thirds of the island is a national wildlife refuge built on land the U.S. Navy used for decades. What they left behind is some of the most pristine coastline in the Caribbean — empty beaches, healthy reefs, and the brightest bioluminescent bay on the planet.

Getting here takes a little effort, and that's the point. The people who make the trip get an island that feels nothing like the resort strip in Condado. Here are twelve things worth the ferry ride.

Getting to Vieques

The public ferry departs from Ceiba (not Fajardo — the terminal moved in 2018). The ride takes about 30 minutes and costs around $2 each way. Tickets sell out fast on weekends — buy them online through the Puerto Rico ferry system as early as possible.

Small propeller planes fly from San Juan (SIG) and Ceiba on Cape Air and Vieques Air Link. Flights take 20 minutes and run $80-$150 one way.

Rent a car on the island. There's no Uber, no public transit, and the best beaches require unpaved roads. Book a Jeep or SUV well ahead — rental inventory is limited.

1. Mosquito Bay Bioluminescent Kayaking

Night Activity 2-3 Hours Book Ahead

Mosquito Bay holds the Guinness World Record for the brightest bioluminescent bay in the world. The water here contains up to 700,000 dinoflagellates per gallon — microscopic organisms that flash electric blue when disturbed. Every paddle stroke, every hand dragged through the water, every fish darting underneath your kayak sets off a burst of light.

Tours run nightly in small groups with certified guides. You'll paddle transparent kayaks through mangrove channels and into the open bay. The best glow happens during new moon phases when the sky is darkest. Book at least two weeks in advance — this is the most in-demand activity on the island and tours sell out consistently.

Swimming in the bay is no longer permitted (to protect the organisms), but the kayak experience is unforgettable.

2. Sun Bay Beach (Sombe)

Beach Half Day Free

Sun Bay is Vieques' main public beach — a wide crescent of golden sand with calm, shallow water and coconut palms for shade. It's the only beach on the island with facilities: bathrooms, showers, picnic tables, and a parking area managed by the local government.

The real draw here is the wild horses. Paso Fino horses roam freely across Vieques, and Sun Bay is one of their favorite spots. You'll see them walking along the waterline, grazing in the grass behind the beach, or standing in the shade of sea grape trees. They're not fenced or managed — they just live here.

Sun Bay is the easiest beach to reach and a smart first stop to get your bearings on the island.

3. Playa Caracas (Red Beach)

Beach Half Day Wildlife Refuge

Red Beach sits inside the Vieques National Wildlife Refuge on former Navy land. The military kept people out for 60 years, and the result is a beach that looks like it hasn't been touched. Clear turquoise water, soft sand, no development in sight — just a few gazebos for shade and a dirt parking area.

The road in is paved and the beach is relatively easy to access compared to other refuge beaches, making it one of the most popular stops on the island. "Popular" on Vieques means maybe 30 people on a busy day. Bring everything you need — there are no vendors, no restaurants, and no lifeguards.

4. Black Sand Beach (Playa Negra)

Beach 1-2 Hours Unique

A small, striking pocket beach with dark volcanic sand unlike anything else on the island. Black Sand Beach is tucked into a rocky cove on the southern coast, and the contrast between the dark sand and bright Caribbean water makes it one of the most photogenic spots in Vieques.

The beach is compact — more of a cove than a stretch — and better for wading and photos than a full beach day. The access road is rough and a high-clearance vehicle helps. Combine it with a visit to nearby beaches for a full south-coast loop.

5. Snorkeling and Diving

Water Sports Half Day Guided or DIY

Vieques sits in some of the clearest water in the Caribbean, with visibility regularly hitting 60-80 feet. The reefs around the island are healthy — decades of restricted Navy access meant minimal fishing pressure and no coastal development on most of the shoreline.

Snorkelers can wade in from several beaches: Playa Caracas has a reef on its eastern edge, and Mosquito Pier (an old Navy dock on the north side) is famous for sea turtles that graze on algae growing on the pilings. Divers book trips through local operators to sites like Angel Reef, The Wall, and Blue Tang Reef.

Hawksbill and green sea turtles are common. Spotted eagle rays, nurse sharks, and the occasional manatee round out the wildlife list.

6. Wild Horses of Vieques

Wildlife Ongoing Free

There are an estimated 2,000-4,000 wild horses on Vieques — Paso Finos descended from Spanish colonial stock. They're everywhere. Walking down the road. Standing on the beach. Grazing in someone's front yard. Blocking the one-lane bridge near Esperanza.

You don't need to plan a "horse activity" — they'll find you. The best encounters happen at Sun Bay in the early morning, along the roads through the wildlife refuge, and near the Esperanza waterfront at dusk. Keep a respectful distance. They're wild animals, not pets, and stallions can be unpredictable.

For visitors from the mainland, seeing horses walk freely on a Caribbean beach is one of those moments that makes Vieques feel like a different world.

7. Esperanza Waterfront (Malecon)

Town Evening Dining & Drinks

Esperanza is the laid-back southern town on Vieques — a short strip of restaurants, bars, and guesthouses facing a calm waterfront. The malecon (boardwalk) runs along the shore and is the island's social hub after dark. Cold Medalla beers, fresh fish tacos, live music on weekends.

Duffy's is the local dive bar that's been here forever. Bananas is the spot for cocktails with a water view. El Blok is a boutique hotel with a rooftop bar and the best sunset angle in town. Walk the whole strip in ten minutes, then pick a spot and stay.

Esperanza is also where most bio bay tour operators pick up, so plan dinner here before or after your kayak tour.

8. Isabel Segunda (Town Center)

Culture 2-3 Hours History

Isabel Segunda is the ferry port and the island's administrative center. It's smaller and quieter than Esperanza, but it holds the most history. The Fortin Conde de Mirasol — the last fort built by the Spanish in the Americas — sits on a hill overlooking the harbor. Inside is a small museum covering Vieques' Taino roots, sugar plantation era, and the Navy's controversial 60-year occupation.

The town square has a few local shops, a panaderia for morning coffee, and the kind of hardware-store-meets-everything that only exists on small islands. The ferry terminal area has improved in recent years, with a few restaurants and a marketplace.

If you're catching a morning ferry back, budget an hour to walk Isabel Segunda before you leave.

9. Vieques National Wildlife Refuge

Nature Half Day+ Free Entry

When the U.S. Navy left Vieques in 2003 after decades of protests, they handed 18,000 acres to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. That's roughly two-thirds of the island — now the largest national wildlife refuge in the Caribbean.

The eastern end is mostly off-limits (ongoing munitions cleanup), but the western and southern sections are open. Drive the dirt roads through dry tropical forest, stop at overlooks, and explore beaches that see maybe a handful of visitors per day. The refuge is home to four endangered sea turtle species that nest on its beaches, plus the Vieques coqui and several endemic lizard species.

The visitor center near the main entrance has maps and current road conditions. Some roads close after heavy rain — check before heading out.

10. Fishing Charters

Water Sports Half or Full Day Guided

The waters around Vieques produce tarpon, bonefish, permit, snook, and barracuda on the flats, and mahi-mahi, wahoo, and tuna offshore. The island sits between the Atlantic and Caribbean, and the drop-off to deep water happens close to shore.

Flats fishing is the specialty here — wading or poling a skiff through shallow turtle grass flats on the south side, sight-casting to tarpon and bonefish. Deep-sea charters run half and full days out of Isabel Segunda or Esperanza.

Local captains know these waters and they're the real deal — no party boats, no tourist traps. Book directly through island operators for the best experience.

11. Playa La Chiva (Blue Beach)

Beach Full Day Wildlife Refuge

Ask anyone who's been to Vieques which beach is the best and the answer is usually Blue Beach. A long stretch of white sand with impossibly clear water in a dozen shades of blue and green. It's inside the wildlife refuge, reached by a dirt road that winds through scrubby forest.

Blue Beach is actually a series of small coves separated by rocky points, each with its own personality. Some are calm wading pools, others have gentle waves. The water is shallow far out, warm, and so clear you can count fish from the shore.

There's nothing here — no shade structures, no bathrooms, no vendors. Bring a cooler, an umbrella, reef-safe sunscreen, and enough water for the day. This is arguably the best beach in all of Puerto Rico, and on a weekday you might share it with five other people.

12. The Island Food Scene

Food & Drink Ongoing Budget to Upscale

Vieques punches above its weight on food. The island's small size means chefs and restaurant owners are deeply embedded in the community, sourcing fish from local boats and produce from nearby farms.

El Quenepo is the fine-dining standout — Caribbean fusion in a restored colonial house in Esperanza. Reservations recommended, especially in high season. Bili is the newer addition that locals love, with creative cocktails and a menu that changes with what's available.

For casual eats, the roadside seafood shacks between Isabel Segunda and Esperanza serve fried snapper, alcapurrias, and empanadillas for a few dollars. The food trucks near Sun Bay fire up on weekends. And every bar in Esperanza will pour you a piña colada made with local rum that costs less than a bottled water at a San Juan resort.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get to Vieques from the main island of Puerto Rico?

The most common way is the public ferry from Ceiba, which takes about 30 minutes and costs around $2 each way. You can also fly from San Juan or Ceiba on small propeller planes with Cape Air or Vieques Air Link — flights run about 20 minutes and cost $80-$150 one way. Book ferry tickets online as early as possible; they sell out on weekends and holidays.

Do I need a car on Vieques?

Yes. Vieques has no public transportation, no Uber, and no taxis to speak of. Many of the best beaches sit at the end of unpaved roads inside the wildlife refuge, so a Jeep or SUV is ideal. Rental inventory is limited on the island — book well in advance, especially during peak season (December through April).

When is the best time to visit Vieques?

December through April offers the driest weather and calmest seas. The bioluminescent bay glows year-round, but it's brightest during new moon phases when the sky is darkest. May through November is quieter with lower prices, though hurricane season runs June through November. Check moon calendars when planning around the bio bay.

Is Mosquito Bay really the brightest bioluminescent bay in the world?

Yes. Mosquito Bay (Bahia Mosquito) holds the Guinness World Record. The concentration of dinoflagellates here — up to 700,000 per gallon — is higher than anywhere else measured. On a moonless night, the glow is vivid enough to see the outline of fish swimming beneath your kayak.

Is Vieques safe for tourists?

Vieques is generally safe and has a small-town feel where locals are welcoming to visitors. Apply standard travel precautions — lock your car and don't leave valuables visible on the beach. Some former Navy areas on the eastern end remain off-limits due to ongoing environmental cleanup. Stick to marked trails and open beaches in the wildlife refuge, and you'll be fine.
