Puerto Rico 7-Day Itinerary: The Ultimate Island Guide (2026)

A week in Puerto Rico is the magic number — long enough to explore Old San Juan, hike a tropical rainforest, kayak a glowing bioluminescent bay, visit two offshore islands, and unwind on the west coast. No passport, no currency exchange, domestic flights. This day-by-day plan covers every highlight without rushing, with tour recommendations, budget tips, and where to stay along the way.

Before You Go

  • Where to stay: Split your nights: 3 in San Juan (Old San Juan, Condado, or Isla Verde), 1 in Fajardo or on Vieques, and 3 on the west coast (Rincón or Aguadilla). This cuts driving time and gives you the full island experience.
  • Rental car: Essential. Pick up at SJU airport day one, return day seven. Expect $40–$70/day. Roads are well-maintained on main highways; rural roads can be narrow.
  • Book ahead: Bio bay tours, Culebra ferry/boat tours, and popular snorkeling trips sell out 1-2 weeks in advance — especially in peak season.
  • Budget: $1,800–$3,500 per person for 7 days (flights + hotels + food + car + tours). Puerto Rico is one of the best-value Caribbean destinations.

Day 1: Old San Juan

Morning

Land at SJU, pick up your rental car, and head straight to Old San Juan. Start at Castillo San Felipe del Morro (El Morro) — the iconic six-level fortress perched on the headland. Walk the ramparts, explore the tunnels and sentry boxes, and watch locals flying kites on the massive lawn. Then cross to Castillo San Cristóbal, the largest Spanish-built fortification in the Americas.

Afternoon

Walk the blue cobblestone streets at your own pace. Calle del Cristo for galleries and shops, Calle Fortaleza for the famous umbrella street photo. Lunch at a local spot — try mofongo (mashed plantains with garlic and pork) and a piña colada at Barrachina, where the drink was invented. Walk the Paseo de la Princesa promenade along the waterfront as the afternoon cools.

Evening

Dinner in Old San Juan — dozens of options from upscale to casual. If it's Thursday through Saturday, end the night at La Placita in Santurce — the market square transforms into the best open-air nightlife in the Caribbean. Live salsa, reggaetón, and cold drinks in the street.

Day 2: El Yunque Rainforest

Morning

Drive 45 minutes to El Yunque National Rainforest — the only tropical rainforest in the U.S. National Forest system. Start early (gates open at 7:30 AM) to beat crowds and afternoon rain. Hike the Juan Diego Falls trail (1.5 miles) to swim in natural waterfall pools surrounded by jungle. Or take the shorter La Mina Falls trail for a dramatic cascade.

Afternoon

Drive the Yokahú Tower road for panoramic views from the 69-foot observation tower — on clear days you can see the coast and offshore islands. Stop at the visitor center to learn about the Taíno heritage and the forest's 240+ tree species. Cool off at Luquillo Beach (20 minutes from El Yunque) — a calm, palm-lined crescent with the famous kioskos food stalls serving alcapurrias, empanadillas, and fresh coconut water.

Evening

Head to Fajardo for the night (15 minutes from Luquillo). Check in, rest up — tomorrow starts early.

Day 3: Bio Bay & Culebra Day Trip

Morning

Take an early ferry or boat tour from Fajardo to Culebra (45 minutes). Head straight to Flamenco Beach — consistently ranked among the top beaches in the world. White sand, turquoise water, no development on the beach. Snorkel the reef at the west end, or just claim a spot and float. Bring your own snacks and water; amenities are basic.

Afternoon

If you booked a snorkeling tour, you'll hit spots like Tamarindo Beach for sea turtles and Carlos Rosario for coral reef diversity. Otherwise, explore the tiny town of Dewey — a handful of restaurants, a couple bars, and that's the charm. Ferry back to Fajardo by late afternoon.

Evening

Tonight is bio bay night. Book a kayak or electric boat tour of Laguna Grande in Fajardo — paddle through a mangrove channel into a lagoon where every stroke lights up blue-green. New moon nights are the most vivid. This is the single most memorable experience on the island.

Day 4: Vieques Island

Morning

Take the ferry from Ceiba to Vieques (30 minutes). This smaller, quieter island is where Puerto Ricans go to escape Puerto Rico. Rent a car or golf cart on arrival — you'll need wheels to reach the beaches. Head to Sun Bay, the main public beach with facilities, or drive the dirt roads into the Wildlife Refuge to find hidden coves.

Afternoon

Explore the former military lands (now a national wildlife refuge covering two-thirds of the island). Playa Caracas (Red Beach) and Playa La Chiva (Blue Beach) are stunning and often nearly empty. The roads are rough — a Jeep or SUV is worth the rental premium. Pack lunch; there aren't restaurants near the refuge beaches.

Evening

Stay overnight on Vieques. If you didn't do the Fajardo bio bay, Vieques has Mosquito Bay — the brightest bioluminescent bay in the world (Guinness-certified). Tours run nightly from Esperanza. Alternatively, have dinner in the tiny waterfront town of Esperanza and catch a world-class sunset from the malecón.

Day 5: West Coast Drive

Morning

Ferry back to Ceiba and pick up your car. Today is a cross-island drive to the west coast — about 2.5 hours via the expressway (Route 52 south, then Route 2 west). Or take the scenic southern route through Ponce and the coast road. If going through Ponce, stop at the Parque de Bombas (the iconic red-and-black firehouse), walk the plaza, and grab lunch.

Afternoon

Arrive in Rincón or Aguadilla and check in. Rincón is the surf town — laid-back, bohemian, with sunset views over the Mona Passage. Aguadilla is slightly more developed with Crash Boat Beach (vibrant local scene, great snorkeling off the old pier). Settle in, explore the town, find a coffee shop.

Evening

Sunset on the west coast is an event. Head to one of Rincón's beachfront spots — The Pool Bar at Villa Cofresí or Tamboo Tavern right on Sandy Beach. Watch the sun drop into the Caribbean with a local Medalla beer. Dinner at a west coast restaurant — seafood is the move.

Day 6: Surf, Snorkel & Caves

Morning

Take a surf lesson in Rincón (beginners welcome year-round, best waves November–March) or head to Steps Beach for some of the best shore snorkeling on the island — reef starts just 20 feet from shore, sea turtles are common. Alternatively, book a whale watching tour if visiting January–March (humpback whales migrate through the Mona Passage).

Afternoon

Drive 30 minutes to the Río Camuy Cave Park — the third-largest underground cave system in the world. A trolley takes you down into a massive sinkhole, and guided tours walk you through cathedral-sized chambers with stalactites, stalagmites, and an underground river. Book timed tickets online in advance.

Evening

Last full night on the west coast. Try a farm-to-table restaurant in Rincón — the west coast food scene has grown significantly. Or head to Aguada for authentic lechón (whole roasted pig) at a roadside lechonera — a quintessential Puerto Rico experience.

Day 7: Beach Day & Departure

Morning

Sleep in. One last beach morning — Crash Boat Beach in Aguadilla for the scene, or Playa Sucia (Cabo Rojo) if you want to make the 45-minute drive south to one of Puerto Rico's most photographed beaches. The limestone cliffs, turquoise water, and salt flats are worth the detour if time allows.

Afternoon

Drive back to San Juan (2–2.5 hours on the expressway). If your flight is late, stop in Condado for a final lunch and walk along the beach. Return your rental car at SJU and head home.

If you have extra time

A late-evening flight? Walk the Condado Lagoon boardwalk, browse the shops at Plaza Las Américas (the Caribbean's largest mall), or grab one last mofongo and rum cocktail in Santurce.

Budget Breakdown

  • Flights: $150–$400 roundtrip (domestic, varies by city and season)
  • Hotels: $700–$1,750 (7 nights at $100–$250/night, mixed accommodations)
  • Rental car: $280–$490 (7 days at $40–$70/day)
  • Food: $280–$560 ($40–$80/day — mix of local spots and restaurants)
  • Tours & activities: $200–$400 (bio bay, snorkeling, caves, surf lesson)
  • Ferries & misc: $50–$100
  • Total per person: $1,800–$3,500

Pro Tips

  • Pack light layers — it's tropical but El Yunque and evening breezes can be cool. A rain jacket is essential for the rainforest.
  • Cash is useful — kioskos, lechoneras, and small-town spots may be cash-only. ATMs are everywhere.
  • Sunscreen and bug spray — reef-safe sunscreen is required at many beaches. Mosquitoes are real, especially near mangroves at dusk.
  • Drive defensively — Puerto Rico driving is aggressive by mainland standards. GPS helps, but road signs can be inconsistent outside San Juan.
  • Learn a few Spanish phrases — English is widely spoken in tourist areas, but a "buenos días" and "gracias" go a long way everywhere else.
